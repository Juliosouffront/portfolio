Every meaningful project arrives with constraints: time, platform, regulation, legacy systems, team size, or simply the limits of what one person can hold in their head at once.

For a long time I treated constraints as obstacles. Now I treat them as collaborators.

## Constraints reduce anxiety

Unlimited options create paralysis. When everything is possible, nothing feels decided.

A constraint gives you permission to stop searching and start building. It turns an infinite field into a path.

## The best constraints are human

The most useful constraints are not arbitrary. They come from real people in real situations.

Can this be understood in three seconds? Can it be completed with one hand? Can someone who has never used the product before know what to do next?

Those questions are constraints dressed as empathy.

## Working inside the frame

When I design within a clear frame, I spend less time defending choices and more time refining them.

The frame does not make the work smaller. It makes the work sharper.

That is what I mean when I say start small and see big — not thinking less, but focusing more.
