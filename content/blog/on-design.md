Design is not decoration applied at the end. It is the shape of care made visible.

When I work on a product, I start with the feeling I want someone to have when they use it: understood, unhurried, confident. Every layout decision, every word, every transition either supports that feeling or erodes it.

## Constraints create clarity

The best work I have done happened inside tight constraints. A limited palette. A single primary action. A screen that had to work for someone standing in line, one hand on a coffee cup.

Constraints force you to decide what matters. They remove the temptation to add one more feature, one more panel, one more option that nobody asked for.

## Care is a design material

Care shows up in small places: the loading state that does not feel like a dead end, the error message that tells you what to do next, the handwritten note inside a box.

These details rarely appear in a case study hero image. But they are often what people remember.

## What I am optimizing for

I am not optimizing for visual cleverness. I am optimizing for honesty — products that feel like someone thought about you, even if you never meet the person who made them.

That is the standard I hold myself to on every project.
