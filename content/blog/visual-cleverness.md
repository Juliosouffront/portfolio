There is a version of design that wins awards and another version that wins trust. They overlap sometimes. Often they do not.

Visual cleverness is seductive. A surprising layout. An unexpected animation. A metaphor so elegant it makes reviewers pause.

But cleverness without purpose is noise wearing a tuxedo.

## The portfolio trap

Portfolio culture rewards the screenshot moment — the frame where everything looks perfect, dramatic, and slightly unreal.

Real products live in messier conditions: bad lighting, interrupted attention, outdated phones, and users who did not opt into your artistic vision.

## Design for the second visit

I try to design for the second visit, not the first impression.

Does someone know where to go? Does the hierarchy hold when they are tired? Does the interface still feel warm when they are frustrated?

If the answer is yes, you have something more durable than cleverness. You have clarity.

## When cleverness earns its place

Cleverness belongs when it carries meaning — when the interaction teaches something, delights without hiding function, or reflects the personality of the product honestly.

Otherwise, simplicity is the braver choice.
