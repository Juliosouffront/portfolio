Momentum is not speed for its own sake. It is the feeling that the work is going somewhere worth going.

Teams lose momentum when decisions pile up without resolution, when feedback loops stretch across weeks, or when every review becomes a referendum on taste instead of a conversation about intent.

## Start with a shared picture

Before pixels, I try to align on three things: who this is for, what moment we are designing for, and what success feels like for that person.

When those are clear, debates get shorter. People stop arguing about button color and start asking whether the flow respects someone's time.

## Ship small, learn fast

I believe in starting small and seeing big. A proof of concept. A single journey. A prototype that tests one assumption instead of ten.

Small releases keep morale high and risk low. They also surface the real problems early — the ones that only appear when something is in someone's hands.

## Protect the details that matter

Momentum does not mean cutting corners on the details people feel. It means being ruthless about what does not matter so you have energy for what does.

That is how you build with momentum and care at the same time.
